Research

Computation and experiment on the same question: how do proteins move, and which of those motions can we use to control them?

Current

2022–2026

Amaro Lab, UC San Diego

MPro: allostery and dimerization of the SARS-CoV-2 main protease

I used molecular dynamics and enhanced sampling (metadynamics and GaMD) to identify allosteric hotspots that govern oligomerization and catalytic activity of MPro. Site-directed mutagenesis experimentally validated the predicted hotspots. I also built analysis pipelines for pocket volume, solvent accessibility, and sidechain rotamers that extract residue-level insight from trajectories.

Biophysical Journal, 2026Split luciferase dimerization assay, JBC 2025

2023–2025

AViDD Consortium, UC San Diego

Interface inhibitors and membrane multiprotein targets

As a research fellow in the Airborne Viral Drug Discovery Consortium, I designed potential interface inhibitors against novel binding sites on viral drug targets, working closely with wet-lab teams to turn structural insight into testable hypotheses. I assembled membrane-bound multiprotein complexes from experimental structural data to simulate them and identify druggable sites.

Past

2020–2022

Burkart Lab, UC San Diego

AcpM and carrier-protein interface engineering

For my M.S. I computationally evaluated acyl carrier proteins from Mycobacterium tuberculosis in mycolic acid biosynthesis. I designed and modeled protein–protein interfaces to engineer hybrid metabolic pathways, then expressed and purified 15N-labeled constructs and used solution NMR titrations to test the designs.

Interface engineering, ACS Chem Biol 2023Interface analysis, Synth Syst Biotechnol 2022

2019–2020

Rotundo Lab, University of Miami

Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors, learning, and memory in mice

I studied how acetylcholinesterase inhibitors affect learning and memory in mice, using visual tracking software for radial arm maze experiments. I also purified acetylcholinesterase from mouse brain tissue for enzyme assays.
